Apex

  • Interaction Design
  • User Interface Design
  • Information Architecture
Role UX/UI Designer
Duration 1.5 months
Team Delivery Manager and UX / UI Designer

Apex lets eye care professionals show presbyopic patients what multifocal contact lenses will look like, before fitting them. The product worked, but it was built around the optics rather than around the appointment: four optional tabs, a printed manual to follow alongside the screen, and clinical calculations left to the operator. Two rounds of usability testing framed the redesign.

The first round gathered feedback from eye care professionals using the existing prototype; the second reviewed the proposed redesign. The result replaces the four-tab structure with a three-step outline, aligned with Johnson & Johnson design standards.

Achievements: Four optional tabs replaced by a three-step guided sequence. Instructions moved from the printed manual into the interface. Twelve prescription options cut to those valid for each patient, and the interface rebuilt on the Johnson & Johnson design system.

Challenge: The software and its printed guide had to be redesigned as one thing. Two people share the session with opposite needs — the professional operates while the patient looks and answers — and professionals disagreed on how much control the tool should offer.

Outcomes

Guided sequence — Three steps replace four optional tabs, with a Continue button in a consistent place and a header that shows where you are.

Fewer, stronger defaults — One recommendation from the fitting guide. Alternatives are presets, and options that do not apply to the patient are hidden.

Instructions on screen — Guidance and patient prompts live in the interface, so the tool no longer depends on a printed manual to be operated.

Built for the technician — Clinical calculation moved into the software, and Right and Left replace OD, OS, D1 and S1 throughout.
